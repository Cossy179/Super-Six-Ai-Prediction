# Experiment 005: Dixon–Coles shape for the market model

- **Date:** 5 November 2026
- **Status:** protocol and promotion rule committed *before* any 2018-19 result was seen
  (experiment 004's runs were still in progress). Results are added below.

## Why (DESIGN §10.4, "Model C")

A residual-dependence diagnostic (`scripts/dependence_diagnostic.py`) on experiment 003's
held-out ensemble forecasts found:

- **no residual home/away goal correlation**: covariance −0.008 (z −0.5) on 2019–22 and
  −0.014 (z −0.9) on 2023–26. A full bivariate Poisson model is therefore **not**
  warranted;
- 0-0, 1-1, 1-0 and 0-1 frequencies within about 1.8 standard errors of forecast;
- **draws slightly under-forecast in both windows**: 26.2% observed vs 25.1% forecast
  (z +2.1) and 25.4% vs 24.3% (z +2.2).

The likely cause is the market model's shape. Two Poisson rates are fitted to four market
probabilities (home/draw/away and over 2.5 goals), and cannot match the draw price. In a
typical example the market prices a draw at 27.95% and the Poisson fit gives 25.57%.
`market_dc` adds a Dixon–Coles ρ, fitted per fixture to the same four probabilities. It
matches them exactly in the example (ρ = −0.11).

## Candidate and rule

- Candidate: `ensemble_dc` = `ensemble` with `market_model: market_dc` (market odds with a
  Dixon–Coles shape, otherwise the same Dixon–Coles fallback).
- **Fresh test: 2018-07-01 → 2019-07-01.** The diagnostic used only the 2019–22 and
  2023–26 forecasts. Reused windows: 2019–22 and 2023–26, as consistency checks.
- **Promote** (`ensemble.market_model: market_dc`) only if all hold:
  1. on the fresh window, its exact-score log loss beats `ensemble`, with the 95%
     interval excluding zero;
  2. on the fresh window, its points per fixture (points mode) is not significantly
     worse than `ensemble`;
  3. on *both* reused windows, its log loss is not significantly worse than `ensemble`.
- Also reported: the draw calibration (forecast vs observed) for both models.

**Decision: fails** (condition 1). `ensemble` keeps the Poisson-shaped market model.

### What we learned

- The two-rate Poisson fit slightly *under*-forecasts draws. Matching the market's
  de-vigged draw price exactly *over*-forecasts them by about the same amount, so the
  proportionally de-vigged draw price itself runs high. Neither shape is better overall.
- Together with the residual-covariance check, this closes the Model C question for now.
  There is no residual home/away dependence to model, and the draw rate is not
  improvable this way. A different de-vig method (for example, one that corrects for
  favourite–longshot bias) would be the next thing to try, under its own predeclared
  test.
